College students are one of the most vulnerable populations when it comes to mental health. 73% of college students experience some form of mental illness during their academic journey. Only 25% of students with a mental health problem seek help. More than 80% of college students feel overwhelmed by their workload.

Depression symptoms include decreased interest in activities, lack of energy, feelings of hopelessness, difficulty completing schoolwork. 

Anxiety often manifests through sweating, trembling, dizziness, or rapid heartbeat. Living with roommates, managing heavy workloads, and developing an independent identity all contribute to anxiety in college students.

Suicide is the 2nd leading cause of death among young people. If you or someone you know is in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide and Crisis Lifeline) or text COALITION to 741741 (Crisis Text Line).

20% of women and 10% of men in college struggle with an eating disorder.

Nearly half of college students meet the criteria for at least one form of addiction. Binge drinking is common on American campuses.

Barriers to seeking help include stigma, lack of services, and lack of information about where to get help.

Self-care tips for college students:
- Go for a walk around campus or nearby trail
- Schedule social time with friends outside of class
- Maintain a consistent sleep schedule — aim for 7-9 hours
- Keep a journal — write things you're grateful for
- Clean your space — a clean room makes for a clear mind
- Practice breathing exercises like box breathing or 4-7-8 breathing
- Try the Pomodoro Technique — 20 minutes of work, 5 minute break

Who to talk to on campus: college counseling services, a trusted professor, your Resident Advisor (RA), or trusted peers.

National resources: 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline (call or text 988), Crisis Text Line (text COALITION to 741741).

The right laptop can make or break your college experience. You don't need the most expensive model — you don't want to spend on stuff you won't even need.

Best overall: Acer Swift 16 AI — $1,199. Light at just over three pounds, 16-inch OLED screen, Intel Core 7 chip, 16GB RAM, 1TB SSD, 17-hour battery life. Great for schoolwork or research.

Best budget laptop: Acer Aspire Go 15 (2025) — $279 at Best Buy. Intel Core i3, 16GB RAM, 512GB storage. Handles research and writing essays without problems. No touchscreen or backlit keys but runs smoothly.

Best battery life: MSI Prestige Flip 14 AI+ — $1,299. Battery lasted 34 hours in testing. Intel Core Ultra X7, 32GB RAM, 14-inch OLED touchscreen. Includes stylus for handwritten notes.

Best MacBook for students: MacBook Neo — $599. A19 Pro chip, 8GB memory, 256GB SSD, 13-inch Liquid Retina display. Great build quality. Most affordable MacBook to date.

Best Chromebook: Asus Chromebook Plus CX34 — $399-549. Fast enough for Zoom, Google Docs, Spotify. 14-inch 1080p screen, solid webcam, 13-hour battery. Not for video editing or heavy design work.

Best ultraportable: Microsoft Surface Pro 2025 — $799. Just over a pound. Great for rushing between classes. 12-inch screen can feel cramped with many tabs open.

Best gaming laptop: Acer Nitro V 16 AI — $799. RTX 5050 GPU, AMD Ryzen 5, 16GB RAM. Handles schoolwork and gaming. Up to 12-hour battery.

How to choose: Think about your budget first. Chromebooks handle email, streaming, and writing papers and cost $200-$1000. For more tasks, 2-in-1 touchscreen laptops are versatile. 8GB RAM recommended minimum — Chrome and Spotify are memory hogs. Pay attention to the keyboard since you'll type a lot.

Chromebooks run Chrome OS and are internet-based. Windows laptops support locally installed programs and can handle gaming and video editing.

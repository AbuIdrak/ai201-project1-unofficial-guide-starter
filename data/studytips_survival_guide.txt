Academics:
Don't fall behind in reading assignments — you'll never catch up.
For math and science, practice problems nonstop. Understanding formulas does not mean memorizing them.
Ask yourself on exams: does this answer make sense? Double-check for careless mistakes.
Go to class. Just like that.
Use the early easy weeks to get ahead before the workload piles up. You will thank yourself later even if you've done just one assignment ahead.

Time Management:
Keep a semester calendar with all due dates and test dates. Review it weekly.
Use a simple weekly schedule to block out classes, study sessions, and life activities.
Budget your time like money — spend it on what truly matters.

Health and Balance:
Sleep before exams — no grade is worth being a zombie. A clear head during exams is worth more than one extra night of studying.
Eat actual food, hydrate, and breathe.
Keep hobbies and social life in moderation to avoid burnout.

People and Resources:
Build relationships with professors, advisors, and classmates. They'll matter more than you think.
Visit professors' office hours and campus advisors. Free help is gold and they are actually willing to help you.
Explore campus clubs and opportunities — you'll gain skills beyond the classroom.
Don't compare your timeline to others. Everyone's path looks different.

Big Picture:
Focus on learning, not just grades. The habits you build matter long-term.
Try a class outside your comfort zone — you never know what sparks new interests.
Your major may not define your future career forever, and that's okay.

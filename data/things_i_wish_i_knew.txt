Get familiar with course selection logistics early. Know the exact day and time you can start picking classes — freshmen have fewer credits so pick times are later.

Have backup classes ready. You need at least 12 credits per semester to remain a full-time student.

Put together a balanced schedule. Avoid making your first semester too challenging — add 1-2 interesting general education classes. If your first semester is too hard, you can get overwhelmed adjusting to a new living environment while keeping up academically.

Use RateMyProfessors wisely. For gen eds where there are multiple instructors, choose someone with good reviews.

It's okay to drop a class. If it isn't required for your major and the workload is unreasonable, drop it. Dropping within the first week or two avoids getting a W on your transcript.

Read the syllabus. You get a sense of course expectations, assignment descriptions, and due dates early on.

Don't buy textbooks until you're sure you need them. Go to class first and hear from the professor that the book is required. Try Amazon for rentals or e-books.

Don't skip class. Missing once is like a gateway drug — it gets easier to justify every time.

Study notes after each class or review all notes at the end of each day. This reduces cramming and helps retention.

Get help from professors during office hours. No matter how small your question, it's better to ask early.

Start projects early. Work on a bit each day rather than cramming before the deadline.

Always do extra credit. Even if you're doing well now, a tough assignment later could lower your grade.

Use a digital calendar. Google Calendar for classes, due dates, and events. Reminder apps for smaller tasks.

Start making connections with professors early — participate in class, ask questions after class. Knowing 2-3 professors well provides job references and recommendation letters.

Get involved in both professional experiences (research, internships) and just-for-fun activities (clubs, intramural sports).

Take advantage of the first few weeks when everyone is looking to make friends. Ask someone their name, major, where they're from. Invite them to join you at the dining hall or library.

Get enough sleep — aim for 7-9 hours. Sleep leads to better academic performance and makes it easier to retain information.

Make your mental health a priority. Meditating, maintaining social support, and having a relaxing nighttime routine all help. Recognize when you might benefit from professional help.

Don't go home every weekend or call your family all the time. College is a stepping stone to living alone — make sure you can be without family while you still have thousands of people around for support.

Your RA is not just there for roommate issues. They're trained to help with homesickness, making friends, and academic struggles.
